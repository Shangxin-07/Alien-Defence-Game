          Who goes

            Everyone eats a unit of food a day. Hungry people work at half pace and stop healing, and after a few hungry days they start to leave. Medicine goes to whoever is too hurt to work and heals them faster. Scrap pays for rooms before money does, at $10k a unit. Every place holds a fixed amount; a bigger team empties it faster but gets no more out of it, and once it's picked clean it's gone. Searching turns up new places, usually smaller ones. Small teams are harder to spot, but a lone scavenger who meets an alien is on their own. The black market sells for cash; every lot you buy pushes its price up, and it takes weeks to settle back. A hydroponic farm feeds five a day, but it needs a lift, power and 12 days to bring in its first crop.

              Promises

                Send someone out

                  Recruiters walk the streets and talk. Most people want something for joining, and the better they are, the more they ask. Keep the promise and they grow loyal. Break it, or let their family go hungry, and loyalty drops; under 30 they walk out, and the bitterest take food with them. Drifters ask for nothing and bring little. People who wait too long for an answer move on.

                        Vehicles

                          With no radar, aliens are found because someone saw one and got word to you. An alien moves on within 8 hours of being seen, so every hour on the road lowers the chance it's still there. Walking covers 5 km an hour; a car covers 40 but burns a unit of fuel every 8 km, there and back. Fixing up an abandoned car takes scrap for parts and your handiest people for days. Win and you bring home the body or the alien, scrap off its kit, and a little goodwill.

                            Loadouts

                              Nobody packs their own bag. Each person asks for a loadout, and the quartermaster fills it from the stores in the order people are listed, as soon as anything changes. People who are out keep what they took with them. All ammunition fits all guns for now: a clip fills any magazine. At home a part-used magazine is swapped for a full one and the rounds go back in the stores. In a fight, reloading drops whatever was left in the old clip. Energy weapons take no clips, but every shot heats them, and fired too fast they shut down for a turn or two. Alien guns can't be issued until they've been researched. Win a fight and you bring your dead's kit home; lose and it stays where they fell. Used medikits are restocked at home for a unit of medicine.

                              Unit allocation

                                Tick what each person is for. Preferred people can still be sent anywhere, but every team list and send button warns you first. Locked people can't be picked for anything else, and their job at home follows the lock: someone locked only to work outside the base rests between trips. Anyone left on Any job goes wherever they're sent. Fights aren't a duty of their own: an ambush finds whoever is out on a run, and a sighting call is a hunt you choose to send people on.

                                Staff & research

                                Research

                                  People

                                    Anyone can do any job. Fewer people means slower work, never no work. Building costs person-days: each builder gets their construction skill's worth done a day, taking jobs in the order they were placed, two to a cell at most. Labs speed research at their base by half again. Anyone under half health rests until they heal, a point a day.
